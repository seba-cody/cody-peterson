— A note from the author —

The book that anchors this site began with a question I could not put down: what kind of shadow falls behind a figure of light? It became a study of the alcoholic — a figure Western culture both elevates and reviles, often inside the same sentence — and of the long, patient apprenticeship to feeling that recovery actually is.

What I have come to think, after a decade of reading Homer and the Jungians side by side, is that we have been working with an impoverished vocabulary for the inner life. The Greeks named twenty kinds of feeling where we now name two. Thumos — a word that meant breath, courage, appetite, temper, the seat of life — vanished into the English word spirit, and we lost the organ along with the word.

Everything on this site — the book, the papers, Seba.Health, the lexicon of five thousand Greek passages — is one long argument with that loss.
